# If I Were a Discretionary Trader — What I Expect From This Platform

Written from the seat of the user this is actually for: a discretionary crypto
trader who makes their own calls, doesn't want a bot trading for them, but knows
their biggest losses come from their own decisions — not from a lack of charts.

---

## Who I am

- I trade my own book. I decide entries and exits. I don't want a black box
  telling me "buy now — 87% confidence." I've been burned by those.
- I already have TradingView. I don't need another charting tool. I have too
  much information and too little discipline.
- My problem isn't finding trades. It's **taking bad ones, oversizing, revenge
  trading after a loss, and holding losers.** My edge leaks through behavior.
- I want a second opinion that is honest, fast, and has a memory of what I did.

## What I open the app for

Not to "analyze the market" for an hour. I have ~2 minutes before I act. I want:

1. **"Is today a day to trade?"** One glance: risk-on / caution / sit out, and
   why. If the tape is chop, tell me to stay small or stay out.
2. **"Is this specific trade any good?"** I have a symbol in mind. Tell me if the
   setup holds, what the risk is, and what would kill it.
3. **"Stop me if I'm about to do something dumb."** If I'm oversized, chasing,
   or trading right into an unlock or a CPI print — say it before I click.

If the app nails those three, I open it every session. If it makes me hunt
through six dashboards, I close it and go back to my own chart.

## What I expect on each

### The daily read
- A straight regime verdict, not 12 gauges I have to synthesize myself.
- Told in words: "Range-bound, low conviction — reduce size, skip breakouts."
- The 2–3 things that are *actually* moving, not a wall of 50 tickers.

### The per-trade check
- Per style: is this a scalp, an intraday, a swing? The answer differs and I
  want it split that way.
- A clear **not-yet / wrong-strategy / here's-what-flips-it** — never a vague
  "neutral." Neutral is useless. Tell me the condition I'm waiting for.
- Risk in terms I use: where's the stop, what's the R:R, what's my invalidation.
  If there's no valid stop, tell me it's an unstructured trade.
- Any catalyst I'm walking into: unlock, listing, funding blowout, macro print.
  I forget these constantly. Surface them **on the verdict**, not in a feed.

### The behavior mirror
- Show me *my* patterns from *my* trades: my worst hour, my revenge-trade tell,
  the setup I keep forcing that keeps losing. I can't see these in the moment.
- One or two habits to fix. Not a 30-metric dashboard I'll never read twice.

## What earns my trust

- **Show your track record.** If you tell me a setup type works, show me it's
  been forward-tested and how it's actually done. Don't just assert.
- **Auditable scores.** If there's a quality score, let me open it and see the
  parts. A number I can't inspect is a number I won't believe.
- **Admit uncertainty.** When conditions are unclear, say so. A tool that's
  confident 100% of the time is lying, and I'll catch it once and never return.
- **Be right about the "no."** The day it tells me to skip a trade that would've
  stopped me out — that's the day I trust it. Protecting my capital earns more
  loyalty than any winning call.

## What makes me leave

- Signals with no reasoning. "Long BTC" with no why, no stop, no invalidation.
- A confidence % dressed up as a probability. I know it's not, and it insults me.
- Noise. Alerts on every headline. If it cries wolf, I mute it and it's dead.
- Auto-trading pressure, or anything that feels like it wants my withdrawal keys.
  The moment I don't trust the custody model, I'm gone.
- Being told I'm wrong with no path forward. "No trade" with no "here's what
  would make it a trade" is a dead end.

## The one thing that would make this indispensable

Every other tool is built to get me *into* trades — that's how they look useful.

**I want the tool that's willing to tell me not to trade, and is right about it.**

Close that loop — honest read of the day, honest read of the setup, an actual
stop-check before I click, and a memory of my own mistakes — and this stops
being another dashboard. It becomes the thing I check before I risk money,
because it's the only one in my stack whose job is to keep me disciplined
instead of active.

That's what I expect. Build for that trader and you don't need to convince
anyone — they'll feel it the first time it saves them from themselves.
